# Medical briefing

Getting to the patient is the job: in tall buildings, the trip from the curb to the patient is about a third of the
response time, and elevators and locked doors are what slow it.

Fact lines, in this order (3 is usual, 4 at most):
1. ELEVATOR: freight car on record (fits a stretcher) or passenger only, from the data.
2. ON FLOOR: the tenant(s) on the patient's floor with suite numbers, so the crew knows which door. Headcount if known.
3. MEET: the property manager or building contact with phone, and whether notify-writer texted them. Never invent a name.
4. Only if tenant-risk flags something on that floor that changes how the crew enters (a lab, medical gases): HAZARD.

NOT KNOWN: AED location and where the stretcher elevator is, unless the data or a crew note gives them. The suite, if missing.

Left for MORE: everything about the building itself (stories, age, frame, sprinklers), hydrants, staging, roof, the floor
map line and the 3D tour. None of it gets a stretcher to the patient faster.
